Do Young Kim

Ph.D. Student in Bioengineering · Intelligent Optics Laboratory · UCLA

I am a Ph.D. student in Bioengineering at UCLA, advised by Prof. Liang Gao in the Intelligent Optics Laboratory. I build optical imaging systems that capture fast biological dynamics in three dimensions, from optical design and instrumentation to computational reconstruction.

My current work centers on squeezed light-field microscopy (SLIM): kilohertz volumetric imaging of neural activity, and its extension to the second near-infrared window (NIR-II) for imaging deep inside living tissue. Before UCLA, I developed non-bleaching super-resolution techniques with gold nanorods at the University of Michigan (M.S.), and I received my B.S. in Information Display from Kyung Hee University (summa cum laude).

research

High-speed volumetric imaging

Squeezed light-field microscopy (SLIM) for kilohertz 3D imaging of neural and cellular dynamics.

Deep-tissue NIR‑II imaging

NIR-II squeezed light-field microscopy for high-speed volumetric imaging deep inside living tissue.

Super-resolution techniques

Non-bleaching super-resolution imaging with gold nanorods, from the phase intensity nanoscope (PINE) to interferometric PINE (iPINE).
