**UCLA Intelligent Optics Laboratory** · with Prof. Liang Gao

Neuronal action potentials and other fast biological signals unfold in three dimensions within milliseconds, faster than a scanning microscope can sample a volume. Light-field microscopy records spatial and angular information together, so a single camera exposure is enough to reconstruct a volume. Squeezed light-field microscopy (SLIM), developed in our lab, takes advantage of the redundancy in light fields and compresses the light field into a narrow region of the camera sensor that can be read out at kilohertz rates. The first SLIM system recorded more than 1,000 volumes per second over a 550 µm field of view and 300 µm depth ([Wang et al., _Nature Methods_ 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02843-8)).

**My work**

- Extending SLIM to volumetric neural imaging in vivo and pushing the readout speed limits of sCMOS sensors for volumetric neural voltage imaging (SLiM 2.0, presented with O. Benshlomo at SPIE Photonics West BiOS 2027).
- Carrying the same principle into the second near-infrared window for deep-tissue imaging: see [Deep-tissue NIR-II imaging](/projects/2_deep_tissue_nir2/).
