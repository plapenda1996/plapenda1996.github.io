**University of Michigan, Bioplasmonics Laboratory** · with Prof. Somin Eunice Lee · 2022 – 2024

Fluorescence super-resolution methods are limited in observation time by photobleaching. Phase intensity nanoscopy localizes gold nanorods instead, which scatter light without bleaching. A liquid-crystal phase-intensity (PI) device modulates the phase difference between field components and converts it into intensity changes. These changes separate nanorods that sit within the same diffraction-limited spot, and the positions are reconstructed with the FISTA algorithm. The phase intensity nanoscope (PINE) resolved sub-10 nm cellular architectures and followed nanoscale reorganization for more than 250 hours. Interferometric PINE (iPINE) carries the approach over to interferometric scattering (iSCAT), using circularly polarized illumination to suppress background fluctuations and resolve neighboring nanometer objects.

**My role**

- Built the iSCAT-based iPINE system and its phase-intensity module (liquid crystal, quarter-wave plate, and linear polarizer under LabVIEW control).
- Extracted nanorod phase from elliptical Airy patterns and reconstructed images with FISTA.
- Built a compact soft multimaterial photonic device (mINC) from liquid crystal, a film polarizer, and a film quarter-wave plate for plasmonic super-resolution imaging.
